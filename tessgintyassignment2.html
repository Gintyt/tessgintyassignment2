System Analysis of The Tower Website

Heuristic Review

For this assignment, I chose to evaluate the website of The Tower rather than the archives. For my topic, I picked Catholic saints. As I was searching the site for information, I encountered some particular strengths and weaknesses of the website. The strengths are as follows:

  • user control and freedom (the menu drops down and is repeated at the bottom of the page).
  • speaks the users language well (recognizable symbols, usual topic categories, etc.).

Here are some visuals of these strengths:

The menu being available no matter where one goes on the page makes for an easy escape button, and the easily recognizable icons for different functions and the commonly used categories for topics ensure that the user will know where to find things. However, this website also has a few weaknesses, including the attrocious aesthetic with images and information everywhere and the lack of help areas. I could not find any FAQ section, no self-training, nothing of that sort. Here is an example of the horrific aesthetic:

The fact that there are images and words and tags everywhere does not bode well for information retention, and may end up hurting the user's eyes. Also, the lack of any help areas mean that when something does go wrong, the user has no way to fix it.

As I was exploring the system, I clicked on multiple different things and got results. Here is the required table:

All in all, this website does a pretty good job when tested by Nielson's Heuristics.

Mini-Usability Test

The Mini-Usability Test went well. My tester immediately went to the oldest possible date for the first task and worked her way back up to the present. She was a little frustrated because our internet was a bit slow to load the oldest articles, but other than that the first task went smoothly. The second task was a bit more difficult in that she was worried that she would not finish on time. I told her that whether she completed the task did not really matter because that was not the point of this, and that got her to calm down. She did find articles that fulfilled the task, so she was pretty happy. As for the questionaire she was asked to fill out afterwards, the only two things that were not marked as "Strongly agree" were "The system prevented me from making mistakes and helped me easily recover from mistakes" and "The system provided informative feedback to help ensure that I understood what just happened." These two were marked as "Neither disagree nor agree," which matches what I observed about the lack of "help" functions present in this system. She also noted "I like how they categorize and label the articles (sports/politics/etc.)," which also matches what I said about the website speaking the user's language.

Based on the results of these two tests, I would recommend that this system adds a "help" section, perhaps in the form of FAQs or an AI help bot. This would allow the users to ask for help when they need it instead of them being left to founder about in the dark.
